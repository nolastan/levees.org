# Writing guide for learn.levees.org

This guide is for anyone — human or AI agent — who writes or edits text on learn.levees.org. Read it in full before you write a word.

learn.levees.org is the evergreen companion to [levees.org](https://levees.org). Levees.org has published more than 800 posts since December 2005, mostly in a blog format. This site reorganizes that body of work into lessons a newcomer can read in order. **Reuse existing Levees.org text wherever you can.** Write new text only to connect ideas from different posts, to explain a term, or to bring an old post's facts up to date. When you do write new text, it should be impossible to tell where Sandy Rosenthal's words stop and yours begin.

---

## 1. Who we are and who we're talking to

Levees.org is an investigative public education nonprofit founded in 2005 by Sandy Rosenthal and her then-15yo son Stanford. Its mission: **to provide education on why the levees broke in New Orleans during Hurricane Katrina.**

The reader is:

- An American who lives in one of the counties protected by levees (62% of the population), who may remember Katrina only as a hurricane.
- A student — high school, college, or engineering school.
- A reporter on deadline who needs the vetted facts and the right words.
- A New Orleanian or flood survivor who deserves to see the record set straight.

Assume intelligence, not prior knowledge. Never assume the reader already knows what a floodwall, a sheet piling or the Army Corps is.

## 2. The voice

Levees.org writes the way its founder speaks at a podium: **plain, declarative, confident, and fact-anchored.** It is an advocate's voice that wins arguments with documents, not adjectives.

### Characteristics

| Trait | What it sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| **Declarative** | "The levees failed because they were not properly designed." Lead with the claim, then prove it. |
| **Short paragraphs** | One to four sentences. A single-sentence paragraph is fine for emphasis. |
| **Specific numbers** | "17 feet instead of between 31 and 46." "$100 million." "62 percent." Never "much shallower" or "a lot of money." |
| **Named sources** | Name the study, the judge, the page number when we have it: "the Decision-Making Chronology (2008), page 4-27." |
| **Names the responsible party** | "the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers," not "officials" or "engineers" or "the government." |
| **Moral clarity, not rage** | Firm and occasionally sharp ("a fairy tale took flight"), but never name-calling. The Corps' failures are "institutional and date back decades"; Levees.org has always said it does not blame individual employees. |
| **Plain-language explanations** | Technical ideas get an everyday comparison: blaming the levee board "was like blaming the janitor if a building fell to the ground." |
| **Survivor-centered** | The people of New Orleans and St. Bernard Parish are never abstractions. "The survivors deserve for everyone, everywhere to know the truth." |
| **Hopeful persistence** | Levees.org signs off with "We're winning." Even hard chapters end by pointing to what changed or what the reader can do. |

### Signature phrases (use them; don't overuse them)

- "the worst civil engineering disaster in U.S. history" — attributed to experts; Levees.org repeats it often.
- "the levee breach event" / "the 2005 levee breaches" / "the federal flood"
- "the full and vetted story"
- "It is instructive to note…"
- "Everyone deserves to know."
- "No one blames the iceberg." (Titanic, Johnstown, Minneapolis bridge comparisons)
- "separate facts from fairy tales" / "the fairy tale"
- "Myth / Truth" pairs (see Myth busters)
- "62% of Americans live in counties protected by levees" — the reason this matters to the reader.

### Things we never do

- **Never use "Katrina shorthand."** Do not write "Katrina flooded New Orleans," "Katrina destroyed the city," "the city was wiped out by Hurricane Katrina," or call the flood a "natural disaster." Write "the flooding during Hurricane Katrina," "the levee failures during Hurricane Katrina," or "when the federal levees failed." The hurricane is the occasion; the levee failures are the cause. (See the lesson [Words matter](../_lessons/words-matter.md).)
- **Never imply the city is "below sea level"** without qualification. Half of New Orleans is at or above sea level.
- **Never blame the pre-Katrina Orleans Levee Board, environmentalists, or residents** for the levee failures. Those are the myths this site exists to correct. You may describe the myths — clearly labeled as myths.
- **Never call the Corps "evil" or attribute malice to individuals.** Describe what the record shows the agency did.
- **Never use emoji, exclamation-heavy hype, or clickbait.** One exclamation point per page, at most, and only in a quoted call to action.
- **Never invent facts, quotes, dates, page numbers, or statistics.** If it isn't in a Levees.org post or a cited study, it doesn't go on this site.

## 3. Words and style

- **U.S. Army Corps of Engineers** on first mention in each lesson, then **the Army Corps** or **the Corps**. (Levees.org uses "Army Corps" most often.)
- **Orleans Levee Board (OLB)**, **Sewerage and Water Board (S&WB)**, **Southeast Louisiana Flood Protection Authority-East (SLFPA-East)** — spell out on first use.
- **Hurricane Katrina** (not "Katrina" alone on first use). **August 29, 2005**.
- **17th Street Canal**, **London Avenue Canal**, **Orleans Avenue Canal**, **Industrial Canal** (Inner Harbor Navigation Canal, IHNC), **Mississippi River Gulf Outlet (MR-GO)**, **Gulf Intracoastal Waterway (GIWW)**.
- **floodwall** (one word) for the concrete-and-steel walls; **I-wall** and **T-wall** for the two designs; **sheet piling** / **sheet piles** for the steel sheets driven into the ground.
- **breach** = the levee or wall broke open. **overtopping** = water poured over the top. They are different and the difference matters.
- **Lower Ninth Ward** (or Lower 9th Ward in quotes), **New Orleans East**, **St. Bernard Parish**, **Lakeview**, **Gentilly**.
- Numbers: spell out one through nine; numerals for 10 and up, all measurements, money, and percentages ("17 feet," "$100 million," "62 percent" or "62%").
- Dates: "August 29, 2005." Decades: "the 1980s."
- Headings: sentence case. Lesson titles may use the levees.org display style — a plain phrase followed by an *italic accent* phrase (e.g. "Why the *levees broke*").

## 4. Facts we state the same way everywhere

When older posts disagree with newer ones, use these. If you update one, update it here first.

| Fact | Standard wording | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deaths | "1,392 deaths from Hurricane Katrina," per the National Hurricane Center's January 2023 update (earlier estimates were about 1,833). This is the storm's total; say levee failures "led to the deaths of hundreds" in the New Orleans area (NHC). | NHC update; [Levees.org, Jan 2023](https://levees.org/2023/01/17/katrina-remains-the-terrible-apex/) |
| Cost | "$186 billion in 2022 dollars, the costliest storm on record" | AP style guide passage |
| Area flooded | "80% of New Orleans and virtually 100% of St. Bernard Parish" | Levee Exhibit |
| Number of breaches | "about 50 breaches" (an Army Corps map shows 52) | Levee Exhibit; *Words Whispered in Water* |
| The fatal shortcut | Sheet piles driven to "not more than 17 feet instead of between 31 and 46"; the switch saved about "$100 million" | Woolley & Shabman, *Decision-Making Chronology* (2008), p. 4-27 |
| How the canal walls failed | They failed with water well below the tops of the walls — at about half the load they were designed to hold. | IPET; Team Louisiana; Water Policy (2015) |
| Who designs and builds | "The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, as mandated by the Flood Control Act of 1965." Local levee boards handle maintenance and operation. | GAO; Flood Control Act of 1965 |
| Immunity | The Corps "could not be held financially liable due to sovereign immunity provided by the Flood Control Act of 1928." | Judge Duval, January 2008 |
| Levee population | "62% of the U.S. population lives in counties protected by levees — more than 201 million people" (FEMA data obtained by Levees.org under FOIA, 2020). The 2009 figure was 55%. | [Counties with Levees](https://levees.org/counties-with-levees/) |
| Elevation | "Half of New Orleans is at or above sea level." | Campanella, Tulane/Xavier CBR, 2007 |
| The retraction | In 2015, J. David Rogers, Raymond Seed and co-authors (with Levees.org's H.J. Bosworth Jr.) retracted the 2006 ILIT conclusion that blamed the Orleans Levee Board. | *Water Policy* 17(4) |

**When sources disagree on a detail** (for example, the exact minute a breach opened or how many feet below the top of a wall the water stood), prefer the wording in the Levee Exhibit text (levees.org/levee-exhibition-full-length-version), which was reviewed by Ivor van Heerden, Stephen Nelson and H.J. Bosworth Jr. If precision would mislead, say "well below the tops of the walls" rather than picking a number.

## 5. Structure of a lesson

Every lesson file in `_lessons/` follows the same shape. Copy an existing lesson as your template.

1. **Front matter** — `title`, `heading` (display version with one `<em>` accent), `order`, `part`, `summary` (one or two sentences, written like a newspaper dek), `image` + `image_alt` + `image_credit`, `reading_time`, `takeaways` (three to five bullets), `further` (links to the original Levees.org posts), `sources` (studies, rulings, articles).
2. **Opening** — two or three short paragraphs that tell the reader why this lesson matters, ideally anchored in a person or a place.
3. **Body** — `##` sections. Keep each section focused on one idea. Use figures from `assets/images/` with captions and photo credits.
4. **A callout or pull quote** — at most two per lesson. Use the myth callout format when correcting a myth.
5. **Close** — one paragraph that hands off to the next lesson.

Use the figure include for every photo, and always credit the photographer exactly as Levees.org did ("Photo/Roy Arrigo"):

```liquid
{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/bellaire-before-after.jpg" caption="6926 Bellaire Drive near the 17th Street Canal breach, before (2000) and after (2005)." credit="Photos/Roy Arrigo" size="wide" %}
```

Callouts use kramdown attribute lists:

```markdown

The Orleans Levee Board forced the Army Corps to build the system that failed.

<span class="callout-label">Truth</span>
…
</div>
```

## 6. Reusing Levees.org text

- **Prefer quoting and lightly adapting** existing Levees.org text over paraphrasing it. Light adaptation means: fixing typos, updating a statistic to the standard wording above, changing "last week" to a date, and trimming. It does not mean changing the claim.
- **Keep attributions.** If a post quotes Judge Duval, Raymond Seed, John Barry, or Matt McBride, keep their name with the quote.
- **Link to the original post** in the lesson's `further:` list so readers can see the primary source.
- **Find source text** in the levees.org WordPress API: `https://levees.org/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?search=<term>` and `https://levees.org/wp-json/wp/v2/pages?search=<term>`. The richest single source is the Levee Exhibit page (`/levee-exhibition-full-length-version/`). Others: Frequently Asked Questions (`/myth-busters-by-levees-org/`), Major levee studies (`/major-levee-studies/`), The Spectacular Failure of the Outfall Canals (`/the-spectacular-failure-of-the-outfall-canals-in-new-orleans-during-katrina/`).
- **Images**: download into `assets/images/` (resize to at most 1400px wide; prefer JPEG), use a short descriptive kebab-case filename, and carry over the photo credit from the original caption.

## 7. Connecting text: examples

Good connecting text is short, factual, and points forward.

> The 17th Street Canal was not the only place where short sheet piles gave way that morning. Three miles east, the same mistake was waiting at the London Avenue Canal — and there, the walls failed on both sides.

> So far we have followed the water. Now we follow the story — because within weeks of the flood, a version of events took hold that the evidence would take a decade to undo.

Bad connecting text editorializes without facts, hedges, or drifts into a generic tone:

> ~~It's important to remember that many factors contributed to this tragic event, and there's plenty of blame to go around.~~ (False balance, and it contradicts the record.)

> ~~Katrina's fury overwhelmed the city's aging defenses.~~ (Katrina shorthand. The canal walls were new — completed only about six years before the storm — and failed below their design load.)

## 8. Before you publish: checklist

- [ ] No Katrina shorthand anywhere (search for "Katrina flooded," "Katrina destroyed," "natural disaster," "wiped out").
- [ ] Every number matches the standard facts table, or cites its own source.
- [ ] Every quote is attributed and appears in a Levees.org post or the cited source.
- [ ] Every photo has a caption and a credit.
- [ ] The lesson's `further:` list links to the original Levees.org posts you drew from.
- [ ] Paragraphs are short. Headings are sentence case.
- [ ] The page builds (`bundle exec jekyll build`) with no warnings.
