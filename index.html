Background: the Army Corps' Authorized Plan of Protection for the Lake Pontchartrain and Vicinity Hurricane Protection Project, 1965.

The short answer

On August 29, 2005, levees and floodwalls built by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers broke in about 50 places, flooding 80% of New Orleans. Many of the walls fell over with the water well below their tops. In the 1980s, the Corps had misread its own load test and driven steel sheet piles 17 feet deep instead of 31 to 46 — saving about $100 million. A federal judge found the Corps responsible. A 1928 law means it never had to pay.
