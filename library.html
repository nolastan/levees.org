In court

  • January 2008 — the LEVEE case. Judge Stanwood Duval, U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Louisiana, places responsibility for the 17th Street and London Avenue Canal floodwall failures on the Army Corps, but dismisses the case under the Flood Control Act of 1928. Lesson →
  • November 2009 — the MR-GO case. Judge Duval holds the Corps liable for flooding caused by its mismanagement of the Mississippi River Gulf Outlet. Affirmed by the Fifth Circuit in March 2012, then reversed by the same panel six months later; the Supreme Court declined to hear the appeal.
  • September 2009 — levee district settlements. Insurers for the pre-Katrina levee districts settle a class action. A settlement is not an admission of wrongdoing.

Books

  • Words Whispered in Water: Why the Levees Broke in Hurricane Katrina by Sandy Rosenthal (Mango, 2020; Anniversary Edition, Turner, 2025). Winner of the Nautilus Award for Journalism & Investigative Reporting.
  • Path of Destruction by John McQuaid and Mark Schleifstein (Little, Brown, 2006).
  • The Storm: What Went Wrong and Why During Hurricane Katrina by Ivor van Heerden (2006).
  • Breach of Faith by Jed Horne (2006).
  • Catastrophe in the Making by William R. Freudenburg, Robert Gramling, Shirley Laska and Kai T. Erikson (Island Press, 2009), which cites the work of Levees.org.
  • Facing Catastrophe by Robert R.M. Verchick (Harvard University Press, 2010).
  • Rising Tide by John M. Barry, on the Mississippi River flood of 1927.

Read Sandy Rosenthal's book reviews at Levees.org.
